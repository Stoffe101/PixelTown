# Game Vision v0.1: A Living Settlement and Adventuring Guild

**Repository codename:** PixelTown. This is a TEMPORARY name, not an agreed final title.
**Stage:** pre-production design, October 2026. **Implementation:** no gameplay exists yet.
**Vision agreed:** a cozy but deep pixel-art fantasy village and expedition simulator, with an incremental/automation flavor.

## The pitch

Build and sustain a charming, autonomous fantasy village. Develop farms, logistics, workshops, smithies and supplies; recruit or train adventurers; craft their weapons and armor; select a party, equipment, supplies, formation and dungeon depth; watch them explore and fight on their own; intervene occasionally with meaningful tactical orders. Cave discoveries return to the village to unlock buildings, recipes, new capabilities and better expeditions.

**A welcoming home above, an adventurous unknown below.** The settlement is a pleasant place to watch and improve. Underground expeditions are voluntary sources of risk, strategy, surprises and better rewards. Both layers should matter equally: the player preferred Option B, with the comfortable feeling of Option A preserved. The game is not an action RPG, a menu simulator, or a copy of NODE//ZERO/Upload Labs.

## The player's actual actions

1. **Observe** real inhabitants walking, working, gathering, farming, carrying and crafting in an evolving 2D village.
2. **Guide** growth by choosing what to construct, where to invest resources and which activities deserve priority.
3. **Commission** equipment at workshops, then choose which inhabitants become guards/mercenaries/adventurers.
4. **Prepare** expeditions with party, gear, supplies, formation, risk policy and specific cave floor.
5. **Watch** autonomous cave movement, encounters, extraction and loot, optionally issuing Retreat, Defend and Focus Target.
6. **Recover** resources and discoveries, treat injuries, unlock recipes and build better infrastructure.
7. **Expand** into new activities, facilities, automated production and deeper expedition challenges without repetitive micromanagement.

## The dual reinforcing loop

The player manages through mouse-first world interaction, without a mandatory controllable avatar. Buildings begin as player-placed blueprints and become real through autonomous villagers fetching materials and constructing them. Visually, the world has a slightly angled-looking top-down style on a **normal square tile grid**, not an isometric projection.

Village production → workshop and smithy → gear and supplies → adventuring party → selected cave depth → autonomous exploration/combat → rare loot and discoveries → new recipes, upgrades, infrastructure and automation → stronger village production and expedition options.

The first player's journey should be understandable without studying a huge manual. A core principle is **there should usually be an exciting next achievable goal**, not just waiting for the next larger number.

## Game pillars

- **Anticipation and payoff:** every major activity should lead to an interesting next possibility: a construction order enables crafting; crafting prepares a risky expedition; a discovery unlocks a visible village improvement. Do not replace this with artificial wait timers.
- **Living and visible:** work has meaningful animation and actual consequences. A blacksmith retrieves materials, works a forge and produces usable gear. Villagers transport loot and tend fields. The world changes.
- **Village/dungeon interdependence:** what the village produces supplies expeditions; what expeditions discover upgrades the village. Dungeon loot must go beyond Sword +2 replacing Sword +1.
- **Autonomous adventuring with preparation and tactical agency:** no mandatory click-by-click combat or direct player-controlled action character.
- **Relaxation and challenge coexist:** village life is mostly cozy, while players voluntarily choose deeper and more dangerous cave floors. Avoid endless unchosen village emergencies.
- **Incremental + automation with choices:** make the settlement increasingly self-sufficient. Automate repeated work, not interesting decisions.
- **An achievable visual language:** stylized storybook-like 2D pixel art with cute, readable tiny adventurers, coherent proportions, modular assets and satisfying environmental animation.

## Why a new project instead of expanding NODE//ZERO

The previous NODE//ZERO project was inspired heavily by Upload Labs and accumulated sophisticated systems and visual UI without proving a unique, compelling game loop. We recognized the risk of designing a better imitation rather than an original game. PixelTown is a **new concept** where the animated world and the player's preparation, crafting, village building and cave rewards create the gameplay identity. Do not inherit NODE//ZERO's code, roadmap, art style or systems by default.

## Example story of play (illustrative, not locked content)

Four villagers struggle to maintain a tiny settlement. The player builds a workshop and commissions basic guard equipment. Two villagers gear up and enter a nearby cave. The player chooses a deeper floor despite a gear score warning, watches an encounter and orders retreat when supplies get low. They return with a rare fungus. Research reveals how to grow it in a village cellar, improving potion production for future expeditions. Their home visibly changes because of their journey.

## Design constraints and non-goals

Not a fast-action RPG. Not a huge management UI with every stat permanently visible. Not a settlement simulator whose only purpose is to get bigger. Not a dungeon clicker that simply generates rewards on a timer. Not a promise to implement an enormous ecosystem, procedurally generated world or elaborate character relationships before a small playable prototype proves fun.

Themed example items, building names, floors, stats, time progression and ending objectives discussed so far are provisional. A design suggestion is **not** a shipped or approved feature until explicitly accepted and tested.
