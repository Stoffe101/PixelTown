# Living Village, Crafts, Environment and Automation

**Status:** agreed concept and possible extensions. No implementation exists yet.

## Purpose of the village

The village is a living home and the logistical base of an adventurers' guild. Inhabitants should pursue sensible jobs without requiring the player to order every step. The settlement must be satisfying to watch in its own right, and its economic purpose is preparing and sustaining adventuring parties.

## Autonomous inhabitants

Potential occupations and activities include:
- gathering wood, stone and wild resources;
- farming, food production and household needs;
- hauling goods from storage to construction, workshops and armories;
- crafting tools and gear, training guards, maintaining buildings;
- recovering after adventures and returning to village life.

Villagers may develop skills, experience and individual strengths over time. A woodcutter could become a blacksmith or guard, subject to the eventual profession system. Individuality is important for attachment but a deep social simulator, relationships and dozens of stats are not early-scope requirements. The player should set **jobs, work orders and priorities**, not individually steer each tiny inhabitant all day.

## Workshop/smithy gameplay

A workshop starts with useful tools and can grow into a forge or smithy. The intended production chain is visible and economically grounded:

1. The player commissions two swords and armor for the upcoming expedition.
2. Recipes check village resources and required discoveries.
3. A worker brings iron, wood, leather or other actual materials from storage.
4. The blacksmith works with visible forging animation and progress.
5. Equipment appears in an armory or inventory.
6. The chosen adventurers equip the real items before leaving.

Weapon, shield, armor, potion and ration categories are illustrative. Exact crafting recipes, inventories, skills and gear slots are undecided. Crafting should eventually support standing work orders, minimum stock targets and automation to remove repetitive clicks.

## Environmental life

The village may contain houses, fields, grass, trees, winding paths, a river/spring, farm plots, a forge and storage. Visible effects such as smoke, moving water, growing crops, windows lighting at night and inhabitants carrying goods can convey a world that lives. 

A preliminary idea for a living-valley simulator involved channeling water to dry fields, then using the same water for a wheel, later automatically operating gates during rain or flooding. **That water/hydrology system was an illustrative earlier concept, NOT approved as mandatory for this game.** The new village/adventure loop has priority.

## Economy and growth

The village and expeditions must depend on each other:
- food sustains villagers and supplies parties;
- basic resources build infrastructure and craft equipment;
- equipment improves expedition reach and survival;
- cave ore, hides, fungi and discoveries enable better gear, new workshops and village improvements;
- improvements increase village output or make previously rare supplies renewable.

An example discussed was **luminous cave fungi** first used for healing potions, then cultivated in a new underground cellar after discovery. Another example was a cave crystal enabling improved irrigation. Such examples demonstrate how loot can transform the village; exact item canon is not yet decided.

Avoid meaningless currencies, isolated production chains and upgrades that only change numbers. Favor tradeoffs: spend iron on village tools or guards' swords, use food for population support or extended expeditions, produce gear or construction materials first.

## Automation progression

The player may eventually set:
- work queues and repeat orders;
- minimum inventory thresholds;
- job priorities and worker allocation rules;
- resource reserves for expeditions;
- policy-driven production and supplies;
- optional repeat-expedition or retreat rules.

Automation replaces chores but preserves the strategic choice of priorities and objectives. Do not build complex policy editors until an actual repetitive problem warrants them.

## User experience

The player should see what villagers are doing, why a forge stopped, what material is missing, which items are ready, and how new resources affect future possibilities. Contextual inspection is better than a permanently crowded dashboard. Keep the miniature world central to the view.

## Prototype boundary

One small village, four inhabitants, basic gathering and hauling, storage, one workshop/smithy and enough crafting to outfit two adventurers. Include one cave resource that unlocks a visible village improvement. Postpone deep farming, seasons, weather simulation, extensive job trees, multiple villages and large factory systems.

## Open questions

Direct building placement versus designations and autonomous construction; resident attributes; food/needs severity; labor allocation; progression pace; housing growth; climate, seasons and day/night significance; multiple facilities and deeper settlement management. Decide using playable evidence.
